# Compliance Mapping

Banking Servicing & Dispute-Resolution Copilot (BC-AAIE-HACK-01): how the
system relates to the EU AI Act, the NIST AI RMF and India's DPDP Act.

**This is an engineering mapping, not legal advice.** Whether and how each
law applies to a real bank deployment is a legal determination for qualified
counsel. The system here runs on synthetic data only and is not deployed.

## How this was checked

Article, section and category numbers below were checked against the source
texts on 2026-09-19, not recalled from memory:

| Framework | Text used | Not checked |
|---|---|---|
| EU AI Act | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as published in the Official Journal, EUR-Lex HTML text | Any amendment or delay adopted after publication; implementing acts and guidelines |
| NIST AI RMF | NIST AI 100-1, AI RMF 1.0 (category-level numbers and the trustworthiness characteristics; sub-category numbers are not cited) | The Generative AI Profile |
| India DPDP Act | The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023), Gazette of India text | The rules made under the Act, which prescribe the form and manner of notice, breach intimation and more |

Each row is marked **Addressed**, **Partial**, **Gap** or **Not applicable**.
Control IDs (`CTL-xx`) refer to `docs/control-catalog.md`; risks (`R-xx`) to
`docs/risk-register.md`.

## 1. EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)

### 1.1 Risk classification reasoning (Article 6)

- Article 6 sets the rules for classifying an AI system as high-risk, by
  reference to the use cases listed in Annex III. In Annex III point 5 (access
  to essential private and public services), sub-point (b) lists AI systems
  intended to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or establish
  their credit score.
- This copilot answers account, fee and policy questions and drafts disputes
  for a human. It does not score credit, decide eligibility for a product, or
  decide a dispute (`docs/model-card.md` sections 4 and 5). On that reading it
  is not a listed high-risk use, and the obligations that apply are chiefly
  the transparency duty in Article 50 and AI literacy in Article 4.
- Two cautions. This is the team's reading, to be confirmed by counsel. And
  the design choice that keeps it out of scope, that no output ever decides a
  dispute or credit outcome, is a constraint on future features (`R-06`).
- Under Article 113 as published, the Regulation applies from 2 August 2026
  (Chapters I and II from 2 February 2025, so Article 4 already applies).
  Later amendments were not checked.
- Articles 9, 10, 12, 13, 14 and 15 are requirements for high-risk systems.
  They are mapped below as good practice, marked *voluntary*, not as
  obligations the system is claimed to owe.

## 3. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

The Act imposes obligations on a **Data Fiduciary**: the party that decides
the purpose and means of processing. For a real deployment that would be the
bank. Here the data is synthetic, so most rows describe design readiness, not
duties the project currently owes.

| Section | Requirement (paraphrased) | Status | How addressed | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s. 4 Grounds for processing personal data | Process personal data only under the Act and for a lawful purpose | Not applicable now | No real personal data is processed; a real deployment needs a lawful-purpose analysis | `docs/model-card.md` |
| s. 5 Notice | A request for consent is accompanied by a notice describing the data, the purpose and how to exercise rights | Gap | No notice is shown. The AI disclosure is not a section 5 notice | none |
| s. 6 Consent | Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, limited to data necessary for the purpose | Gap | No consent capture. Long-term memory stores facts a customer states without recording consent to that storage (`R-10`) | none |
| s. 7 Certain legitimate uses | Processing without consent is allowed for listed uses | Not assessed | Which listed use, if any, would cover servicing and dispute handling is a legal question left open | none |
| s. 8(3) | Ensure completeness, accuracy and consistency of data used to make a decision affecting the Data Principal | Partial | Dispute answers are drafts a human decides; answers are grounded in tool output; hallucination and faithfulness measured | `reports/eval_report.json`, `src/guardrails/output_risk.py` (CTL-09, CTL-22, CTL-25) |
| s. 8(4) | Implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to observe the Act | Partial | Technical controls catalogued and evidenced; no organisational measures | `docs/control-catalog.md` |
| s. 8(5) | Protect personal data with reasonable security safeguards to prevent a breach | Addressed (technical) | PII masked in inputs, outputs and logs; tool calls limited to the authenticated customer; secrets and PII scanners over the repository and all evidence | `reports/pii_scan.json`, `reports/secrets_scan.json`, `logs/agent_actions.jsonl` (CTL-01, CTL-05, CTL-07, CTL-08, CTL-17, CTL-18) |
| s. 8(6) | Intimate the Board and each affected Data Principal of a personal data breach | Gap | No breach-notification process. The audit trail would support detecting and scoping an incident; the approach is described in `docs/security-approach.md` | `logs/agent_actions.jsonl` (CTL-10) |
| s. 8(7) | Erase personal data when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is served, unless retention is required by law | Gap | No retention or erasure job for the memory store, logs or traces (`R-17`) | none |
| s. 8(10) | Establish an effective mechanism to redress grievances | Partial | Requests the copilot cannot handle hand off to a human agent; there is no formal grievance channel | `src/agents/escalate.py` |
| s. 11 Right to access information about personal data | Data Principal can obtain a summary of data processed and the processing activities | Gap | No access mechanism | none |
| s. 12 Right to correction and erasure of personal data | Data Principal can have data corrected, completed, updated or erased | Gap | No correction or erasure mechanism; stored memories cannot be edited by the customer | none |
| s. 13 Right of grievance redressal | Readily available means of grievance redressal | Gap | As s. 8(10): human hand-off only | `src/agents/escalate.py` |
| s. 14 Right to nominate | Data Principal can nominate another individual to exercise rights on death or incapacity | Not applicable now | Not implemented; relevant only to a real deployment | none |
| s. 9 Processing of personal data of children | Verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data | Not applicable | Out of scope; no age handling exists | `docs/model-card.md` |

## 4. Summary of gaps

Rows marked **Gap** or **Partial** that matter most, and where they would be
addressed in a real deployment:

1. **Consent, notice, access, correction, erasure and retention (DPDP
   ss. 5, 6, 8(7), 11, 12).** The project has no data-principal lifecycle. A
   real deployment needs a consent and notice flow and a retention and erasure
   process covering the memory store, logs and traces.
2. **Breach notification (DPDP s. 8(6))** and a grievance channel (ss. 8(10),
   13): process, not code; see `docs/security-approach.md` for the approach.
3. **Output marking (AI Act Art. 50(2))**: unimplemented; applicability to be
   confirmed.
4. **Fairness and explainability (NIST MEASURE 2)** and a **feedback loop
   (MEASURE 4)** are not evaluated or built.
5. **Accuracy (AI Act Art. 15, voluntary)**: the measured 0.6 predates three
   fixes, and re-scoring is outstanding.
